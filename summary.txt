World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026

Iran attacked ship-navigation systems on Oman's Didamar Island in the Strait of Hormuz, Tehran said; Reuters could not confirm damage. Marco Rubio said US pressure made major combat unnecessary, the New York Times reported, but warned of force if Iran crossed 'red lines'. Tehran vowed wider war and said no enriched uranium would leave Iran.

In Abu Dhabi, Zelenskyy called day one of Ukraine-Russia-US talks 'constructive and quite substantive', and officials said they agreed a large prisoner exchange; NATO jets scrambled as drones strayed into Baltic airspace.

Israel's new Knesset was sworn in after a deadlocked election left Netanyahu with no majority and no government, as Eisenkot moved to control the agenda. Gaza health officials said Israeli forces killed seven people, including a 13-year-old.

The Wall Street Journal reported Nvidia's board was preparing to remove CEO Jensen Huang, after the New York Times reported an alleged affair with executive Kimberly Powell. Shares fell about 8% after hours to $159. An inquiry had opened days earlier, after Huang admitted an affair with a woman US intelligence suspects of working for China.

Prosecutors charged three alleged Iranian agents over about 20 plots in the US and Europe, one at RAF Fairford; Treasury chief Scott Bessent moved to a military base after threats, the New York Times reported. Democrat Scott Colom beat Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi, Reuters projected, becoming the first Black senator there since 1874 and giving Democrats a 55-44 Senate lead.

At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Guterres warned slow climate action put the world 'on a path to destruction'. British police arrested a US Army officer, 41, on suspicion of spying for Russia, the BBC reported. In Brooklyn, a building collapsed into a pit, killing at least two and injuring over 10, Reuters reported; Bulgaria fog crashes killed 12; Britain's top court ended Trinidad and Tobago's centuries-old gay-sex ban.
